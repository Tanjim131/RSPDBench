Benchmarks score models on clean products. Deployment feeds them whatever the processing chain delivered.

Before a model sees a satellite image, the light has passed through optics and detectors, and the bands have been calibrated, resampled onto one grid and, for products like HLS, harmonized across sensors. Each step can leave a residual defect.

Broad domain-shift benchmarks change geography, season and sensor at once, so they cannot say which defect caused a failure. RSPDBench keeps every scene fixed and changes one product defect at a time, at three severities, in the product's own units, before any model-specific preprocessing.

Paper Sec. 3.4

Checked against the sensor itself

Sentinel-2 measures near-infrared twice: band B08 at 10 m and band B8A at 20 m, over almost the same wavelengths. Passing the real 10 m band through P1's moderate setting reproduces the real 20 m band of the same scene. Across 3,000 test patches, the best-fitting blur width is σ = 0.75–1.5 pixels, bracketing the benchmark's σ = 1. The real misregistration between the two bands is only 0.02–0.05 px, so P3's 1–2 px levels are deliberate stress tests.

Paper Sec. 4

What breaks, and for whom

Seven frozen foundation models and two supervised baselines on five datasets: Sentinel-2 classification (m-EuroSAT, m-BigEarthNet), crop-type and land-cover segmentation (m-SA-crop, m-Chesapeake), and burn-scar mapping (HLS Burn Scar). Every number is a drop relative to the same model's clean score, averaged over three seeds.

Paper Sec. 4.5

Robustness rankings do not transfer

Rank the foundation models by how much they lose to resolution loss (left) and to a calibration gain error (right), at the severe setting. The two orders are anti-correlated on four of five datasets. RAMEN, which conditions on resolution, loses least to blur on every dataset and most to gain on every satellite dataset; DINOv3 is the reverse. A benchmark built only from blur and noise would miss this.

Paper Figs. 4 and 5

When defects compound

The acquisition chain C1 applies a gain error, blur and coarse-grid noise together. Its excess is the drop beyond its worst single component. It peaks when blur has weakened a model without saturating it; at the most severe setting there is little left to lose.

Paper Sec. 4.7

A smarter readout helps, but not with blur

Retraining only the linear readout on degraded training views, with the encoder still frozen, removes most of the noise and harmonization loss on m-BigEarthNet. Resolution loss still costs about a fifth of the clean score: the detail is gone from the encoder's features. The readout saw the test defect types during training, so this is the best case for defects you can anticipate.

Models and clean scores

Each foundation model runs under its own author-style frozen protocol with a clean-trained light head; clean scores anchor every relative drop. They are not a cross-model ranking, because heads and input contracts differ.

Run the degradations on your own model

The operators work on any (bands, height, width) tensor in native product units. Apply them before your model's own normalization, keep the head trained on clean data, and report the drop from your clean score.

# pip install git+https://github.com/TODO/RSPDBench
import rspdbench as rb

contract = rb.get_contract("hls-burn-scars")
bands = ["BLUE", "GREEN", "RED", "NIR_NARROW", "SWIR_1", "SWIR_2"]
for cond in rb.conditions_for(contract, bands):        # 37 conditions
